# Proposals — audit of the instruction documents, 2026-08-03

**What this is.** A proposal document, not an implementation. Nothing here was applied.
Basis: `STYLE.md`, `docs/combat-design.md`, `STORY.md`, all seven debt records, the
System 4 audit, both System 5 reviews, the System 4 pass-4 review, the project brief and
inspiration notes — and the delivered pixels: `s4-p5-parry-contact`, the three `s3b-p1-parry`
variants, `s3b-p1-wide`, `s3b-p1-knockback`, `s5-p2-fold-plan`/`-strike`, against reference
images 1, 3 and 6 (including `ref3-matched-378.png` at matched scale), the faces corpus and
the texture grids.

**Cost legend.** *cheap* = hours to a day. *medium* = days. *structural* = a system's worth
of work or a decision that moves multiple systems.

Where a proposal contradicts a live rule, the rule is named in **bold** at the end of the item.

---

## 1. The verdict in one paragraph

The project is closer to beautiful than its own debt ledger suggests — the s4-p5/s3b parry
frame has a sky, a mood and a blade language that genuinely belong to the corpus, the no-text
interface survived two cold reads, and the review culture is the strongest apparatus I have
ever seen attached to a picture — but it is not on course, and the single thing most in the
way is the documents' own incentive gradient: the rubric's falsifiable clauses (edges, floors,
lags, corridors) decide PASS/FAIL mechanically, so they become work orders, while the two
things that actually decide whether a frame is a picture — **how much figure resolves** (9–13
parts against the corpus's ~25) and **what the composition is when nobody is striking** (the
planning frame, "a diagram on a nice background", worse in pass 2 than pass 1) — are
unfalsifiable at authoring time and so have never once headed a builder's brief in seven
systems. The hands, grips, guards and folds that every review since System 1 names as the
largest gap have *never appeared on any pass's work order* (System 1 debt D1, still true
today); meanwhile four passes were spent on a corridor statistic that never discriminated
anything and five on a cloth scalar whose final verdict was "no scalar grades this cloth."
Playability is a second, independent absence: six systems, no input handling, and the three
open design questions (`§3d.3` cooldowns, facing economy, lane/queue tuning) all block on "needs
a playable fight" by the documents' own admission. The fix is not more rules — it is moving the
part count and the §0 answer to the head of every brief, splitting the measurement doctrine out
of the visual bible so `STYLE.md` is about ink again, and building the input loop next, before
any further rendering pass.

## 2. What I saw in the pictures

Honest readings at 1× viewing scale unless stated, compared against the references at
matched scale where relevant.

### `s4-p5-parry-contact/frame_010.png` — the flagship
**Genuinely good:** the sky ramp is of the corpus — indigo through violet to coral, correctly
desaturated, and the record's claim that this is the first colour script belonging to the
family is visibly true. The clash star sits on the steel, the embers drift rather than spray,
the ground band darkens toward frame bottom. At arm's length the frame has real mood.
**What fails, in order of visual damage:** (1) the huge pale **crescent trail** hanging in the
sky over the foe's head — a translucent sail a third of the frame tall; reference 3's clash
has *no trail at all*, only the star; it is the single largest non-corpus object in the frame.
(2) The foe's body is a jumble of disconnected dark flaps — the right arm reads as a torn
flag, the legs as sticks; nothing connects. (3) Neither figure has a hand, grip or guard —
both blades emerge from sleeve-mass, so the crossing floats. (4) The shed flecks near the
hero's back read as hard chips of debris, not ink smoke — nothing *curls*; every Family B
reference has smoke streamers coiling off the silhouettes. (5) At matched scale
(`ref3-matched-378.png`) the reference still resolves faces, both hands on grips, hakama fold
lines and ground-contact splash; the capture resolves none of these. The 25-vs-9 gap is not a
statistic, it is the first thing the eye notices once the mood wears off.

### `s3b-p1-parry-contact` vs `-bareface` vs `-inherited`
On `frame_011.png` the faces exist: the foe carries a pale profile with a dark brow line and
streaming hair, the hero a shadowed socket under the bun. The head no longer reads as an
artefact (the pass-4 "block lattice" is gone), and that is a real win. But at contact-sheet
scale I **could not reliably tell the three variants apart** — the visible difference between
bareface and inherited is essentially nil at delivered size, and the face reads mostly as
"the head is now coherent" rather than "there is a face". The corollary of `STYLE.md` §4b.0 is
confirmed by my own eyes: at this framing the face is worth a few parts of legibility, and the
push-in is the only place it can ever be more.

### `s3b-p1-wide`
Frame 0 is the indictment in one image: two figures ~120 px tall in the bottom-left corner of
an otherwise empty gradient. Nothing else in the frame. Frames 2–5, as the camera arrives, are
**among the best pictures the project has produced** — figures large, the foe's hair thrown,
the ochre sash glowing at contact. The camera *arrives* at beauty and *starts* at nothing.
Compare reference image 6 (Family C, the planning mood's stated source): its calm is not
emptiness — the frame is dense with fog banks that are *coloured masses* (rust, coral,
violet), grass stalks, motes, a foreground figure at two-thirds frame height and a background
figure half-dissolved. Sky is perhaps 15% of that image. The delivered wide frame is ~80% sky.
The documents asked for "figures small, heavy fog" and got exactly what they asked for; the
corpus does something different.

### `s3b-p1-knockback`
The carry reads: the body tilts and is carried rather than launched, hair streams ahead along
the travel, and the settle is soft. This is §7.2 working. Two blemishes: the pale figure is
the brightest thing in the frame throughout (a luminous wraith on a dusk stage — striking,
but the inverse of the corpus's pale duellist, which is darker than its ground), and detached
dark chips float near the head mid-carry, reading as debris.

### `s5-p2-fold-plan/frame_003.png` — the graded planning frame
Not a picture, and I say that having wanted to disagree with the reviewers. Three specks on a
horizon strip; the upper 60% of the frame is empty gradient; the stanza glyphs float in the
sky and are effectively unreadable at 1× (the cold reads in the reviews were performed at
3×–14× magnification); the two vermillion strikethroughs — the information that decides
whether banking is safe — took me several seconds to find. One accidental grace note: the
ochre charge ticks at the right margin sit on the horizon line and read as distant lanterns.
It is atmospheric and it is not information. The bottom third is a near-black mottled band.
As the screen a player will stare at for most of every fight, it is simultaneously the least
beautiful and the least legible frame in the project.

### `s5-p2-fold-strike` (contact sheet)
The push-in singles (frames 1, 8, 9) are the best figures in the project: skirt panels
separate, the silhouette is dynamic, the blade reads. The interface recession works — by
frame 8 the margins have gone quiet and nothing floats over the duel. The faded cartouche
ghost in the upper-left sky is visible in the wide frames and does read as a smudge on a
camera lens rather than a mark on a sheet.

### The references, read for what the captures lack
Image 1 (Family A): the density machine — armour lacing, gauntlet, two swords, sash knot,
ochre thigh bloom, splash at the feet, escapee hairs. Image 3 (Family B): figures fill ~60%
of a portrait frame; a dim red sun disk anchors the warm pole *behind the exchange*; ink
smoke curls off every silhouette; the clash is a tight star, trail-less. Image 6 (Family C):
calm as density, described above. Faces corpus: carnation with grey-violet shadows, the eye
as the contrast peak — none of it deliverable at 20–30 px, exactly as §4b.0 already says.
Texture grids: shibori, pleats, rivet rows, temper line — of which only the sash has ever
reached a delivered frame.

## 3. Proposals for beauty and poetry — ranked

### B1. Make the part count an acceptance with a floor, and give the fittings their own work order — **structural; the highest-impact item in this document**
The record is unambiguous: the matched-scale gap (~25 vs 9, maybe 13 with faces) has been the
standing failure since System 1, it is the first thing a viewer notices, and the items that
close it — hand + grip + guard cluster, second sheathed blade, sash knot, fold structure,
feet with ground contact — are mesh authoring that **no pass has ever been briefed to do**
(System 1 debt D1: "Never appeared on any pass's work order"; still true after 3b). Faces
were the right first move; the other ~9 parts are cheaper than faces and mostly static
fittings. Propose a dedicated fittings pass (call it 3c's first act or a System 6) whose
acceptance *is* §11.0's count.

Before (`STYLE.md` §11.0, after "Corollary"):
> **Corollary:** a material can only ever be as good as the subject it is painting. If the
> count is short, fixing the material is refinement of the wrong thing.

After (append):
> **And from the next system on, the count is an acceptance, not a diagnostic.** A delivered
> duellist resolves **at least 18 readable parts** at matched scale against the corpus's ~25,
> and five of them exist by name before any are counted: the hand+grip+guard cluster (three
> small high-contrast marks — they are what tell the eye where the body ends and the weapon
> begins); a second sheathed blade or equivalent back/hip fitting; the sash knot; fold
> structure in the lower garment; and feet with a ground-contact mark. A pass that leaves the
> count under the floor may not spend budget on material refinement, and the count is taken
> by someone who did not produce the work.

Contradicts no rule; it promotes an existing diagnostic. Cost: the rule is cheap; paying it
is structural (mesh authoring, the one discipline the project has never scheduled).

### B2. Rewrite the planning framing: density at constant figure size, not pull-back — **structural, and an owner decision (see §7)**
Three passes have named the same `Stage` change; two declined it. The reviewer's verdict
("diagram on a nice background") and my own reading of `s3b-p1-wide` frame 0 agree, and
reference 6 shows Family C's calm is *density at low contrast*, not emptiness. The fix is not
"more fog dial": it is that the wide shot should never exist as delivered.

Before (`STYLE.md` §9):
> - **Planning framing:** the full lane readable, figures small, heavy fog, Family C
>   mood. Slight slow drift — the camera is never perfectly still.

After:
> - **Planning framing:** framed on the *action*, not the lane. The camera holds the hero and
>   every body that can reach it within the banked horizon, and the hero never drops below
>   ~0.4 of frame height. The rest of the lane is **stated, not shown**: lane marks running
>   off-frame, a vermillion bleed pinned to the frame edge for any off-screen intent, a
>   fog-dissolved value shape for a distant body. Fog is additive — drifting bands and
>   coloured fog-bank masses per §6 — never an attenuation of the subject (System 5 pass 2
>   measured what attenuation buys: the part count went *down*). Family C's calm is density
>   at low contrast: the planning frame carries *more* marks than the execution frame, all of
>   them quiet. Slight slow drift — the camera is never perfectly still.

Before (`combat-design.md` §1.6, "Camera consequence"):
> **Camera consequence.** The planning framing must fit the lane, so a 5-tile lane is already
> near-intimate while a 15-tile lane is genuinely wide.

After:
> **Camera consequence.** The planning framing fits the *reachable action*, not the lane.
> Lane length changes how much of the world is stated off-frame by marks and fog, not how
> small the figures print. The push-in stays small on a short lane and large on a long one,
> measured from the action framing.

**Contradicts** `STYLE.md` §9 ("the full lane readable") and `combat-design.md` §1.6's camera
consequence as written, and partially §1.6's "what the long end buys" (the approach can still
be dramatised — by bodies entering the stated off-frame region, not by shrinking the hero).
The known cost (an enemy off-frame in the planning shot, System 5 §4.5's arithmetic) is the
*point*, not the objection: a linear lane makes off-frame threat representable by a mark at
the frame edge plus its lane tile. Cost: structural (`Standing` into `Stage.planning()`,
schedule fingerprints guarded — the reviews already scoped it).

### B3. Kill the trail during holds; gate it on tip speed — **cheap/medium**
The delivered arc is 0.69 fh wide (audit C1) and at 1× it is a pale sail over the duel. The
corpus's clash frames carry **no trail**. The tip-path fix is already implemented (audit C1c);
what remains is temporal: the ribbon exists whenever history exists.

Before (`STYLE.md` §5):
> - **Motion:** a swung blade leaves a soft luminous arc-trail that fades over ~0.4 s.

After:
> - **Motion:** a swung blade leaves a soft luminous arc-trail that fades over ~0.4 s, and
>   the trail exists **only while the tip is fast**: its alpha follows tip speed through a
>   hard gate, so a held, binding or parrying blade carries no trail at all. In the corpus's
>   own clash frames there is no trail — only the star. Transverse to travel the ribbon is
>   never wider than ~2 blade widths. A trail that outlives its swing reads as a sail, and a
>   sail over the focal point is the loudest wrong object a frame can carry.

### B4. Decide the pale duellist's register: corpus-dark or canonical wraith — **cheap to decide, medium either way; owner decision (see §7)**
Debt item 14 grades the pale foe against the corpus (should be darker than its ground, cool)
and it fails on sign and hue. But the delivered luminous wraith is *striking*, and the enemies
are literally Charted Shadows — dream-things. Either: (a) obey the corpus (3c's value work as
already planned), or (b) canonise the wraith with an explicit STYLE clause ("a Charted Shadow
is the one figure lit from within — pale core, dark rim, never brighter than the clash"), and
retire item 14. What is corrosive is the current state: an accident being half-graded against
a rule it half-breaks, pass after pass.

### B5. Measure the ink-floor ratio now and deepen the figures on the dusk stage — **cheap, and the largest contrast payoff available for its cost**
Debt item 1 is fully specified and nobody has done the half-day: reference 3's dark duellist
prints 12.2 against a ground-band median of ~36 (fraction ≈ **0.34**); a figure obeying the
current floor prints 27.9 — more than twice the corpus, while compliant. 41.6% of ref 3's
lower third sits below the current floor. Run the same fraction on images 4 and 5 (per
§11.0's "one image is not the corpus"), fix the band, and amend §2.2:

Before (`STYLE.md` §2.2, the long amendment note ending):
> The next pass to touch the palette owes that fraction, measured, with the region recorded.

After:
> On a staged ground, the ink floor is **F × the stage's own ground-band median luminance**,
> where F is the Family B band measured across images 3, 4 and 5 through recorded regions
> (image 3 alone reads 0.34; the other two are owed before F is fixed). `#161A22` stands
> only on paper grounds. On the dusk stage this licenses the figures to roughly halve their
> floor, which is most of the remaining contrast gap the ground-third analysis decomposed
> (system4-debt §2.3).

### B6. Ink smoke that curls — wisps as garment-scale escapees — **medium**
Every Family B image sheds curling smoke streamers off the silhouettes; the capture sheds
discrete chips. The one mechanism this project has had judged *poetic* — the hair: tapered
Verlet ribbons with escapees — is exactly the right tool, at garment scale.

Proposed addition (`STYLE.md` §3, after item 2 "Wet bleed"):
> 2b. **Smoke wisps.** Off the trailing edges of garments, a handful of thin curling ink
> streamers — the hair-escapee mechanism at garment scale: 2–5 tapered ribbons per figure,
> sub-pixel at the tip, each on its own slow clock, shed hardest on direction changes
> (§7.3's vocabulary). A figure that only flakes discrete flecks reads as chipping paint;
> the corpus's figures *smoke*.

### B7. Give the Family B stage its warm pole: the sun disk — **cheap**
Reference 3's composition is anchored by a dim red sun behind the exchange; the delivered
stage has a uniform coral band. One radial wash, positioned behind the camera's action focus,
would give the clash a counter-light and the frame a warm centre of gravity. Proposed as an
addition to §6 ("Wash blooms in the ground plane" gains a sibling: "**One low warm bloom in
the sky** — the extinguished sun, always behind the action, never brighter than the horizon
line it sits on").

## 4. Proposals for legibility of the interface and the actions — ranked

### L1. Codify the cold read as the interface's acceptance, at stated magnification — **cheap, doctrine**
The two most valuable results in Systems 5's record are cold reads, invented ad hoc by
reviewers. Make them protocol, and make the magnification honest: the pass-1 and pass-2 reads
were performed at 3×–14×. A mark legible only above 2× is not delivered.

Proposed addition (review protocol, wherever §11 lands after the split in §6 below):
> For glyph or interface work, the review's second act (after the matched-scale comparison)
> is a **cold read**: name every visible mark's meaning from delivered pixels before reading
> any code or document that states it, record the magnification used, and record misses as
> findings. A mark that requires more than 2× to identify is not delivered at that
> resolution.

### L2. Re-author the retreat around refusal, and fix the hand column's allegiance — **cheap/medium**
The record: Back-step read cold as "a thrust or cut travelling left" — an attack — because its
loudest element is a long tapering stroke, and *anything that tapers to a fine tip reads as a
blade in this alphabet*. The carriers of "retreat" (hook, scuff) sit at 28% and 24% of the
travel stroke's peak.

Before (`combat-design.md` §2.2):
> | **Step / Back-step** | Move one tile | Weight transfer |

After:
> | **Step** | Move one tile forward | Weight transfer: short mark where the weight leaves, long mark where it arrives — the long mark points **at the enemy** |
> | **Back-step** | Move one tile away | Refusal: the loud mark is the *withdrawing weight* — heavy, settling, pointing away and slightly **down** (grounded). Nothing in a movement mark may taper to a blade-like tip: in this alphabet a tapered tip means steel. |

And the composition half (pass-2 review §1.4): the hand column was read as *the enemy's
intent* because it sits between the two Shadows above their health row. Move the hand to the
hero's side of the sheet (below the stanza), or bind it to the hero with the hero's own ochre.
Cheap, and it is a placement bug, not a glyph bug.

### L3. The Feint: loud in shape, empty in body — **cheap**
Measured invisible (peak lift 4.5; 17× fainter than its neighbour; fainter than an empty
slot). Drawing "motion with no contact" as *faintness* was the mistake; draw it as **reserve**:

Before (`combat-design.md` §2.2):
> | **Feint** | Free-play reposition, cooldown penalty | Motion with no contact — the negative space that makes contact read |

After:
> | **Feint** | Free-play reposition, cooldown penalty | Motion with no contact, drawn as **reserve**: a full-amplitude gesture whose body is paper — a stroke-shaped hollow in a faint wash, the one mark in the alphabet that is loud in outline and empty inside. A Feint drawn merely faint reads as nothing (measured: peak lift 4.5 over bare). |

### L4. Give Thrust the one thing a gesture cannot mime: its reach — **cheap**
Nobody has read Thrust cold unaided; it reads as footfalls. The tile's identity is *two tiles
ahead*, and the alphabet has no tile metric. Add one: a faint tile-pitch tick crossed by the
stroke. This also gives Step/Draw/Sweep a shared spatial grammar for free. Proposed rewording
of the Thrust row's choreography cell: "Blade passing *through* — the stroke crosses a faint
tile-boundary tick and continues past it, which is what no movement mark does."

### L5. Raise the telegraph at planning framing — **cheap**
The vermillion strikethrough — the single datum that decides whether banking is safe — is
barely findable in the graded planning frame (0.278% of frame, thin over dark ground). §8
already licenses vermillion to be spent freely here. Propose: the telegraph's amplitude
scales with framing width (the same dial as the haze, opposite sign), so the wider the shot
the louder the threat marks — they are the one interface element that must win at distance.

### L6. Keep the countability rule; generalise it to "one glance, one fact" — **cheap, doctrine**
The countability rule (a counted mark countable at every shipped resolution) is the best
interface doctrine the project has produced. Its generalisation belongs in §8: every element
answers exactly one planning-glance question (what resolves next; how many remain; where is
danger), and any element answering none is decoration and must yield. This is the licence to
remove clutter later without relitigating §8.

## 5. Proposals for playability and feasibility — ranked

### P1. Build the input loop next. Before any further rendering pass — **medium, and the highest information-per-effort item left**
Was render-first the wrong order? For this project's stated bet (procedural interaction
animation as the whole point, per the brief) — no; the first five systems bought the thing
the project exists for. But it is the wrong order *from here*, for three reasons the record
itself states: (1) `combat-design.md` §3d.3 lists two open design items and says both "need a
playable fight to settle and neither should be guessed at now"; the queue/lane pairing of
§1.1a and the combo economy carry the same caveat. (2) Every legibility claim is a proxy —
cold reads by reviewers grade *recognition*, not *use under pressure*; whether LIFO-by-
reading-downward survives actual play is unknowable from stills. (3) The engine is already
headless, event-streamed, and 96-tests-deep; the interface renders every state. Wiring
keyboard input (bank / reorder / remove / execute / turn) plus a minimal loop is days, not a
system. Propose it as the next system, with an explicit non-goal: no new rendering work, no
new instruments — the deliverable is a fight a human can lose, and one page of notes on where
the design surprised its first player.

### P2. Specify the interactive tempo — the seam nobody has designed — **cheap to write, prevents a structural surprise**
The engine's stream is deliberately ordinal (no seconds) and STYLE demands settles of
0.3–0.6 s, held breaths, camera glides with a 0.25 s floor, and phrases with long recoveries.
Nobody has added it up: a five-tile stanza against three enemies at §7.1's proportions is a
long, uninterruptible cinematic, every turn. That may be right — the project is a poem — but
it must be *chosen*, not discovered at first play. Propose a new `combat-design.md` §5:
planning is untimed; an execution phrase resolves in ≤ N seconds wall-clock (pick N by
prototype); enemy phrases share one budget; the player may always interrupt the *camera*,
never the *resolution*; and a fast-forward exists from day one (a poem you cannot re-read at
your own pace is a cutscene).

### P3. Cut the second hero from the first milestone — **negative cost**
"One excellent encounter" (combat-design's own scope decision) does not need two heroes. The
Warden/Pilgrim pair exists to prove the interaction layer generic — a fine goal that doubles
the choreography surface while the *first* figure still has no hands (B1). Keep the Pilgrim
(swap is the harder, better test and the named signature), park the Warden.
**Contradicts** `combat-design.md` §2.1's stated purpose ("proved generic rather than fitted
to one case") — deliberately, as a sequencing change, not a design reversal.

### P4. An interactive-rate gate before more shader octaves — **cheap**
The brief names mobile as a target; no frame of this project has ever rendered at interactive
rate — everything is offline capture. Before 3c adds material classes with per-class octave
stacks, run the duel scene live at 60 Hz on desktop and record the frame time. If it misses,
that is a fact the material system must be designed around (fewer octaves, baked fields), and
learning it after 3c is the expensive way.

### P5. Settle the stage geometry once: `TILE_WIDTH` vs `FIGURE_HEIGHT` vs `BODY_HALF` — **structural decision, small implementation**
Debt item 8, named by three passes running, blocks three things at once: base separation
(1.16–1.29× the corpus, audit C11), the planning framing (B2), and the push-in arithmetic.
The audit's correction matters: reaching the corpus needs `LANE_SPREAD` ≈ 1.22 equivalent,
not 1.05, and the honest fix is the `Stage` constant, not the spread mitigation. This is a
combat-design decision (how far apart two combatants on adjacent tiles stand) wearing a
renderer constant's clothes. One decision, three systems unblocked.

## 6. What to remove or restructure in the documents themselves

**The answer to the hard question: yes — `STYLE.md` is no longer a document that helps
someone make a beautiful game.** It is two documents interleaved, and the beauty half is now
the minority shareholder: of ~1,145 lines, §11 alone is ~334 and the measurement doctrine
embedded in §7.1 is another ~100+ (the anchor rule, the rigid control, four generations of
drape-excursion archaeology, the reach-gate instrument spec), plus the floor amendment in
§2.2 and the frequency post-mortem in §3 — roughly **40% of the visual bible is about proving
things about pictures**, and §11.2b(f) alone (guard epistemology, ~115 lines, four separate
clauses about how to write a test) contains not one sentence about ink. Every clause was
earned by a real defect and none should be *deleted* — but a builder opening this file to
learn what a hem should look like now wades through the epistemology of assertions to find
out.

**Does the rubric select for passes that measure well rather than look good?** Not by its
rules — §0 and §11.0 are looking-first — but by its gradient, and the record shows it in both
directions. For: System 3 spent four passes on cloth scalars and concluded "no scalar reduced
from a rectangle grades this cloth" — a verdict available to the eye at pass 1; System 4's
corridor criterion consumed parts of four passes and "has never passed a single band on any
capture" while never discriminating between two settings; System 5 pass 2 delivered
atmosphere as a *corpus-calibrated attenuation* that made the project's own part count go
down — it measured well and looked worse, and the reviewer said so in those words. And the
top-impact beauty item (the fittings) has never been briefed in seven systems, because it has
no number attached. Against: the matched-scale test — a measurement — is what exposed the
part gap at all; the forced-choice pairing is measurement *design* that restored looking; the
reviews genuinely look. So the doctrine is not the disease; the allocation is. The
restructure below plus B1's acceptance floor and the brief rule (last item) are the cure this
audit proposes.

### R1. Split `STYLE.md` — **cheap, high leverage; do it in a quiet window between passes**
- `STYLE.md` keeps §0–§10: ink, light, colour, faces, blades, atmosphere, motion, UI, camera,
  anti-patterns. Target under 500 lines.
- New **`docs/MEASUREMENT.md`** receives: all of §11.2/§11.2b/§11.3, §7.1's instrument
  doctrine and statistic archaeology (anchors, rigid controls, drape history, the reach
  gate), §2.2's floor-derivation narrative, §3's frequency post-mortem. Organised by
  discipline (apparatus / statistics / guards / regions), with the four-level guard ladder
  (observed red → adversarial instance → runs everywhere → skips are failures) stated once,
  compactly, instead of accreted.
- `STYLE.md` §11 becomes ~25 lines: the five required review items, matched-scale-first, the
  cold read (L1), record-the-region, capture-at-true-rate — each one sentence with a pointer
  into `MEASUREMENT.md`.

### R2. Current-rule-only sections, history to a changelog — **cheap**
The live rubric is append-only: §2.2 carries three paragraphs explaining how its own line was
wrong for four systems; §8's queue bullet embeds a two-paragraph amendment with its own
litigation history; §7.1 contains four superseded statistics narrated in sequence. For a
*grading* document this is noise a grader must parse and a place for contradictions to hide.
Each section states the current rule; the "learned the hard way" narratives move to an
appendix or `MEASUREMENT.md` case studies, linked. (The narratives are valuable — that is
why they should be somewhere they can be read on purpose instead of in the way.)

### R3. Fix the two known stale facts in live rules — **cheap**
- `STYLE.md` §8 still opens "the action queue is three ink cartouches"; five are delivered
  per `combat-design.md` §1.1a. After: "…the action queue is a base-anchored vertical column
  of up to five ink cartouches (newest at the top, resolving first)…"
- `STYLE.md` §10's row "UI panels, bars, boxes, borders — Chrome; breaks the paper illusion"
